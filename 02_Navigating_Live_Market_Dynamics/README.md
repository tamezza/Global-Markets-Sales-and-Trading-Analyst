# Part 2 — Navigating Live Market Dynamics

**Status:** ✅ Done

## Task brief

A new client, **Horizon Capital** (Renewable Energy 40% · Technology 30% · Real Estate 20% · Cash 10%, moderate-high risk tolerance), asks for urgent guidance after a volatile session. The goals are short-term agility and long-term growth with reduced volatility. The task has two parts:

1. A 5-question quiz on a live market snapshot (sector performance, sentiment, VIX).
2. A **Market Advisory Email** covering the key trends, the portfolio implications and prioritised recommendations.

## My approach

1. Read the snapshot against the VIX bands in the Live Market Indicators Guide: all readings were in the 15–25 range, so targeted changes rather than a defensive overhaul.
2. Weighted each sector's move and volatility by the client's allocation. Technology holds 30% of the capital but about **39% of the volatility-weighted exposure**.
3. Built a fully funded reallocation, with a staged entry into Renewable Energy and clear conditions for redeploying cash.
4. Compared three allocations in `analysis.ipynb`: current, my proposal and Forage's example answer, then stress-tested them against three shocks (tech sell-off, renewable policy shock, rate rise).
5. Wrote the email in LaTeX for the repo and as Markdown to paste into Forage, with an appendix of charts and tables generated by the notebook.

## Key takeaways

- **Proposed mix:** Renewable Energy 45 / Technology 22 / Real Estate 20 / Cash 13.
- On this snapshot, the weighted return goes from +0.80% to +1.02% and the weighted volatility (VIX) from 17.0 to 16.1. Technology's share of volatility drops from 39% to 30%.
- **Every recommendation needs a trigger:** saying when to act again (for example, VIX above 25 or sentiment turning) is what makes advice "agile".
- **Check that the numbers add up:** Forage's example answer moves weights without saying where all of them go, so its allocation sums to 95%.
- **Stress test:** cutting Technology reduces the loss in a tech sell-off (−1.10% vs −1.50%) and in a rate rise (−1.51% vs −1.70%). The trade-off is a larger loss in a renewable policy shock (−2.70% vs −2.40%), which is why the email caps Renewable Energy at 45%.
- This is a single snapshot: the figures describe it and are not a forecast.
