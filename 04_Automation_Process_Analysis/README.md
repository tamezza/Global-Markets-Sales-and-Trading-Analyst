# Part 4 — Innovating Through Automation or Process Analysis

**Status:** ✅ Done

## Task brief

Find and fix a bottleneck in a trade workflow. The task has three steps:

1. Spot the inefficiency in five client and internal workflows (quiz).
2. Read an urgent request from **Apex Global Investments**, a high-frequency trading client with $1.5bn of annual volume. Its trades take **90 minutes** from capture to report, with a **12% reconciliation error rate** and about **$500k of missed opportunities last quarter**.
3. Write a **Process Improvement Proposal** using the provided template.

## My approach

1. Mapped the 4-step workflow from the Client Profile: data entry 15 min, cross-system validation 20 min, compliance approval 25 min, report 30 min. Separated the steps that delay **execution** (the first three, about 60 min) from the one that only delays **reporting**.
2. Identified the root cause as the chain of manual hand-offs before execution: compliance approval is the longest step, and validation is where the errors come from.
3. Built a **Monte Carlo simulation** of 10,000 trades in `analysis.ipynb`, comparing today's workflow with the automated one: a reconciliation engine, automated reports, rule-based compliance pre-checks and automated trade capture via API.
4. Measured the impact phase by phase and ran a cost–benefit analysis with a sensitivity range.
5. Wrote the proposal in LaTeX, following the template's 6 sections, with every figure and table generated by the notebook.

## Key takeaways

- **Find what delays the trade, not just the longest step:** report generation is the longest step (30 min), but it happens after execution. What makes the trade wait is the 60 minutes of manual work before it.
- **Simulated impact:** average time to execution drops from about 63 to about 6 minutes (−90%), and the error rate from 12% to about 2%.
- **Phase it to fit limited IT resources:** Phase 1 (reconciliation and reports) runs on data Apex already exports and alone takes the error rate from 12% to 2%. The API integration comes last and is managed by the vendor.
- **Show the uncertainty:** the payback is 3–11 months depending on how much of the time saved turns into recovered trades. Even the cautious case pays back within a year.
- **Derived numbers beat asserted ones, but stay credible:** Forage's example promises 90 → 45 min with no calculation. My simulation is more transparent but more optimistic, so the best pitch promises the conservative target and shows the simulation as the upside.
- **Compliance stays in control:** routine trades are approved automatically against rules the compliance team writes, with an audit trail, and officers review only the exceptions.
